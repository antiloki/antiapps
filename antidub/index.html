How it works

Load a book. Render a chapter. Export.

Every step is a small, tested skill that runs on your machine. One chapter renders at a time, with a live preview, and any chapter can be run again.

01

Load any book

Plain text, Markdown, USFM or EPUB. antiDub splits it into chapters and sentences, and keeps the title, author and language you set.

02

Render one chapter

A local voice reads it with human pacing: pauses at sentence and verse ends, a slightly varied pace, loudness levelled to −18 LUFS. A local image model paints the scenes. Watch it build, then re-roll an image or re-run the chapter.

03

Export a pack

Video (MP4) or audio (M4A), two SRT files, WebVTT, a sync.json timeline, the text and the cover. Files you keep, in open formats.

Your book. Your voice. Your machine.

Two ways to dub

A painted film, or an audiobook.

Pick per book, and switch any time. Both come from the same narration and the same sentence timings.

Synced subtitles

Every sentence, on the second it is spoken.

The timings come from the narration itself, not from guessing afterwards. So a player can highlight the line being read, scroll the text in time, or jump to a verse.

Privacy and ownership

Nobody's server reads your book.

anti-cloud

It all runs here

The voice (Kokoro), the pictures (Stable Diffusion through stable-diffusion.cpp) and ffmpeg all run on your computer. Once the models are on disk, it works offline.

anti-subscription

No account, no meter

No sign-up, no credits per minute of audio, no monthly plan. Render a chapter ten times if you want.

anti-tracking

Your files, your disk

Books, renders and the database live in ~/.local/share/antidub. Nothing is uploaded. Exports are MP4, M4A, SRT, VTT and JSON you can open anywhere.

Before an imported book can be exported, you confirm it is public domain or that you own the rights. Settings pick the voice and point antiDub at your own voice and image engines.

What runs today

Honest about where it is.

  • 0.1.1 is a Linux x86_64 pre-release: a .deb and an AppImage. Windows and macOS were not built.
  • Kokoro narration is built in. Download its two model files once; until then you hear a labelled preview voice.
  • Pictures need sd-server (stable-diffusion.cpp) and the DreamShaper 8 and LCM-LoRA weights. A GPU makes it quick; on a CPU, expect about 35 seconds per image.
  • Video and audio modes, synced SRT, WebVTT and sync.json, chapter re-runs, re-rolls and Cancel
  • Import of txt, Markdown, USFM and EPUB, with title, author and language
  • Portuguese books (voices Alex and Dora) and English books today. Other languages are not tested yet.
  • Scenes are planned by built-in rules. A local language model planner is next.
  • A companion viewer app, with watch mode and read-along, is planned.
